## Engineering session 2026-09-21 ~00:45 PDT — dual-scale "1-step Lloyd"

**Picked:** unchecked backlog item "'1-step Lloyd' dual-scale variant"
(the symmetric twin captured 0.84–0.87 in the previous session; this
asks whether one fit iteration also captures the dual-scale win on
skewed tensors).

**Landed:** commit `7eae088` (+ roadmap `...`). Suite 88/88 green (+7).

**Candidate D-dual — `ternary_1step_ds` (`src/quant_rnd/schemes.py`):**
`_ternary_lloyd_fit` with dual=True, n_iter=1 — heuristic dual thresholds
(conditional means per side) -> one per-side L2-optimal refit ->
reassignment. Same 1.835 bpw at group 128 as ternary_lloyd_ds
(registered in _DUAL_SCALE_SCHEMES so reconstruct() uses both scales).
n_iter exposed for the convergence check.

**Numbers (seeds 7–9, clean + skew 0.5, 64 groups, capture of
ternary_lloyd_ds's win over dual_scale_ternary):**

| seed | skew | n=1 | n=2 | n=3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 0.0 | 0.83 | 0.96 | 0.98 |
| 7 | 0.5 | 0.73 | 0.84 | 0.87 |
| 8 | 0.0 | 0.85 | 0.97 | 0.99 |
| 8 | 0.5 | 0.78 | 0.89 | 0.90 |
| 9 | 0.0 | 0.55 | 0.63 | 0.65 |
| 9 | 0.5 | 0.57 | 0.67 | 0.76 |

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** One iteration does NOT replicate the symmetric
twin's 0.84–0.87 capture; the dual case converges slower. 2–3 fixed
iterations (still O(1), no convergence loop) get 0.63–0.99 — so the
practical dual-scale encoder is 2–3 iterations, not 1. Seed 9 skew 0.5
is the slowest (needs n=5 for full convergence) — a slow-convergence
case worth understanding when adaptive stopping is designed.

**Scratch-methodology note:** my first scratch measurement ran the dual
scheme under an unregistered QuantResult name and reconstruct() fell
back to single-scale decoding, underestimating the dual 1-step by
~0.1 capture. The landed numbers use the registered name. Lesson: the
_DUAL_SCALE_SCHEMES registration is load-bearing for correctness —
worth a comment, which is now in the scheme docstring.

reference (ternary_1step_ds n=2, per-side zero-rates).

## Engineering session 2026-09-21 ~01:15 PDT — dual fitted opcount re-run

**Picked:** unchecked backlog item "re-run the opcount energy/speed
comparison with the dual fitted reference (`ternary_1step_ds` at
n_iter=2, measured zero-rate per side)" — the direct continuation of the
previous session's dual 1-step result.

**Landed:** commit `f02df67` (+ GitHub mirror 653d455a78e9). Suite 90/90
green (+2 tests).

**`side_fractions()`** (`src/quant_rnd/opcount.py`): per-code fractions
{pos, neg, zero} for ternary-family QuantResults; raises on non-ternary
codes instead of silently mis-measuring. Rationale: a dual-scale sparse
kernel accumulates the two nonzero sides under separate scales, so the
honest op input is the per-side profile, not just total sparsity.

**`test_dual_fitted_ternary_opcount_reference`** (seed 7, 64 groups,
g128, n_iter=2): decode ceiling ratio 1.272x vs int2_kmeans_q8 (lower
than symmetric 1-step's 1.357x — byte-driven, dual carries 1.835 vs 1.710
bpw); energy-proxy ratio 3.53x clean / 3.40x skewed (vs symmetric's
3.52x). Dual zero-rates: 0.441 clean, 0.418 skew 0.5. Per-side split on
the skewed tensor: pos=0.415 / neg=0.168 — the asymmetric op profile the
re-run was meant to capture; a test pins pos > 2x neg. The dual's extra
scale multiply (n_scales=2) costs ~0.016 muls/weight — genuinely slight.
Bottom line: the ternary compute case holds with the dual fitted
reference; the numbers barely move, as predicted.
